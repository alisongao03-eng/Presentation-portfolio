TALK 01 · SCIENCE · RESEARCH · AI AND DISCOVERY

The New Scientific Revolution

Reimagining Human Creativity and Discovery in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

Duration

40–45 minutes; Lite and Talk editions available

Audience

Scientists, research leaders, postgraduate and undergraduate students, science-literate general audiences

Previously delivered

International science forum · Hong Kong · September 2026

Deck

39 slides · 16:9 · English · Renaissance illuminated-manuscript visual style

Abstract

Twenty years ago, few would have believed that a machine could predict the structures of 200 million proteins, solve Olympiad mathematics problems and generate new scientific hypotheses. Today it has — and the Nobel Prizes have already spoken.

This keynote argues that AI is the newest instrument of discovery in a lineage that runs from the telescope and the microscope to the computer. In seven acts it examines the evidence — AlphaFold and the transformation of biology, materials designed rather than searched for, the emerging AI co-scientist, AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry at the International Mathematical Olympiad, and FunSearch's constructions that no human had found.

Running through the evidence is a central paradox — we can now verify results that we do not yet understand. The talk then turns to the human response: open science as the engine of discovery, four human superpowers — curiosity, imagination, judgment and wisdom — and the TRUST framework for AI-assisted science. It ends with a New Scientific Compact and a message to young scientists: the breakthroughs of this century will come from trusted partnerships between human curiosity and machine capability.

Key themes

  • Science 1.0 to 4.0 — four ages of discovery
  • AlphaFold, AlphaProof, FunSearch and the AI co-scientist
  • Verification vs understanding
  • Open science in the age of AI
  • Four human superpowers and the TRUST framework

TALK 02 · GOVERNANCE · POLICY · PUBLIC ADMINISTRATION

Governance in the Age of AI

Transformation for Human & Global Impact

Duration

45 minutes (adaptable to 30–60)

Audience

Policy makers, public administrators, regulators, university and civic leaders

Previously delivered

International governance conference · Bangkok · August 2026

Deck

38 slides · 16:9 · English

Abstract

Intelligence is becoming abundant. Like electricity a century ago, AI is turning into ambient infrastructure embedded in every sector of society — and the challenge is no longer whether AI will become intelligent, but whether our institutions will become wise.

This keynote tells a story in three acts: why this moment matters, what the evidence says, and how institutions should respond. It introduces the Triple Governance Challenge — governing AI, governing with AI, and governing AI-enabled societies — and the distinction between smart government (doing things better) and wise governance (doing the right things), with trust as the new infrastructure every AI system must cross.

It then maps the global landscape in three waves — the EU's regulatory model, the US/OECD risk-and-standards approach and Singapore's practical assurance — and shows how readiness can be measured from city to sector, drawing on UNESCO's Readiness Assessment Methodology and international maturity indices. A university is presented as a living laboratory for AI governance: AI literacy for all, metric-aligned KPIs and the Triangle of Success of knowledge, skills and attitude.

The talk closes with four human questions for global digital cooperation — agency, trust, inclusive prosperity and social cohesion — and a simple conviction: algorithms will not write our future; we will.

Key themes

  • Smart government vs wise governance
  • Three waves of global AI governance
  • Trust as infrastructure: transparency, accountability, inclusion, ethics
  • Measuring AI readiness with international indices
  • A university as a governance laboratory

TALK 03 · STUDENTS · INSPIRATION · CHARACTER AND SUCCESS

Dare to Fail, Dare to Dream

Finding Your Own Path — success is what people see; failure is what built it

Duration

45 minutes, with live audience interaction

Audience

Undergraduate and secondary students, orientation and inauguration assemblies, commencement, youth and alumni events

Previously delivered

College inauguration assembly · Hong Kong · September 2026

Deck

30 slides · 16:9 · English · hand-painted watercolour illustrations, step animations

Abstract

What if everything you have been told about success is wrong? In this candid, personal talk, the speaker rethinks the word everyone chases — moving from the world's scoreboard of GPA, salary, title and status to a better definition: becoming the best version of yourself and, together, making the world better than you found it.

Then comes something rare on a stage: the speaker tells the story of his own failures honestly. The paper rejected many times before winning Best Paper. Seventeen years to professor. Personal mistakes, missed opportunities and initiatives that did not work. Below the waterline of every visible success sits an invisible mass of rejections, setbacks and doubt — and the conclusion is simple: the most successful people are not those who failed the least, but those who learned the most from their failures.

From those stories come five lessons that success could never have taught — resilience, humility, empathy, lifelong learning and self-discovery — and five practical messages for students: don't chase grades alone, explore beyond your major, fail early and often, build relationships, and use your gifts to make a difference. The talk opens with a live word-cloud poll and closes with three quiet promises: dare to dream, dare to fail, dare to make a difference.

Key themes

  • Two definitions of success
  • Famous failures — and the speaker's own
  • The iceberg: what people see and what built it
  • Five lessons from failure
  • Knowledge, skills and attitude — the successful learner

TALK 04 · EDUCATION · ASSESSMENT · LEARNING ANALYTICS

Data-Rich, Insight Poor?

Reimagining Assessment in the Age of AI

Duration

45–60 minutes; modular sections for shorter slots

Audience

Educators, teaching-and-learning and assessment leaders, quality-assurance and accreditation bodies, EdTech community

Previously delivered

Education keynote series 2026 (current deck)

Deck

63 slides · 16:9 · English · interactive ice-breaker poll

Abstract

Universities have never held more data about their learners — yet the transcript still tries to summarise years of human learning in a single document, and most assessment captures only a snapshot of what a student can do at one moment in time. This keynote asks how AI and learning analytics can turn abundant data into genuine insight about learning.

It begins with how we got here: a brief history of assessment from oral disputations to standardised accountability, the structural weaknesses of the standard paradigm, and eight dimensions of learning — from critical thinking to collaboration and personal growth — that no transcript records. Mapping AI onto Bloom's six levels shows where models excel and where human judgment still matters most.

The second part shows how AI and learning analytics meet these challenges — reducing time and effort, personalising support and early intervention, scaling what one teacher can see, and improving fairness and objectivity — organised by a four-question analytics framework and a five-level maturity model. Drawing on a suite of university-built platforms for integrity, learning and assessment, it sets out four practical approaches for institutions. The vision: assessment that is continuous, timely, personalised, adaptive, scalable, fair and cost-effective.

Key themes

  • Weaknesses of the standard assessment paradigm
  • AI across Bloom's taxonomy
  • Learning analytics framework and maturity model
  • Academic integrity — student, teacher and institutional views
  • University-built platforms for integrity, learning and assessment
